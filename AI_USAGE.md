# Rules for using an AI assistant

I use assistants — a coding dojo tool for guided practice, and a chat model for explanation.
They make learning much faster and they make *not* learning much faster too. These are the
rules that keep it the first one.

## The line

**I never commit a line I cannot explain, from memory, on a blank file, tomorrow.**

Everything below follows from that.

## Allowed

- Explaining a concept I am stuck on, as many times and as many ways as I need.
- Generating *exercises* for me, and generating the tests that check them.
- Reviewing my solution after I have written one and telling me what is wrong with it.
- Explaining an error message, after I have read it myself and formed a guess.
- Rubber-ducking a design decision, with me making the call.

## Not allowed

- Generating a solution before I have attempted the exercise. The struggle is the mechanism;
  skipping it feels like progress and is not.
- Pasting generated code into `solutions/` without retyping it and changing it.
- Asking for the answer when I am tired rather than when I am stuck. These feel identical
  in the moment and are completely different.
- Letting an assistant write a module's `README.md`. That is the part that does the teaching,
  and the teaching is for me.

## The check

At the end of every module, close everything and rewrite one exercise solution from scratch.
If I cannot, the module goes back to in-progress and the assistant does not get consulted
about it again until I have.

## A note on the dojo tool

Guided-practice tools are best used for *drilling* something I have already understood, and
worst used for first exposure — they are very good at making me feel fluent at a keyboard in
a way that does not survive a blank editor. Read or build first, drill second.
